# Model Card — Fetal Head Circumference Measurement

> ⚠️ **Research prototype. Not FDA-cleared. Not CE-marked. Not a medical
> device. Not for clinical use, clinical decision-making, or diagnosis.**
>
> Nothing this system outputs is a clinical opinion. Every measurement,
> gestational-age estimate, reliability verdict and generated explanation is
> intended for research and demonstration only, and requires review by a
> qualified clinician before it informs anything.

**Model family:** FetalScan AI · four checkpoints (2 static, 2 temporal)
**Maintainer:** Tarun Sadarla · University of North Texas · 2026
**Licence:** MIT (code). Model weights derive from HC18 — see *Data* below.

---

## Intended use

**In scope.** Research and demonstration of automated fetal head-circumference
measurement from a single 2-D transventricular ultrasound frame: segmenting the
calvarium, fitting an ellipse, converting the perimeter to millimetres using
supplied pixel spacing, and deriving a gestational-age estimate via the Hadlock
1984 regression.

**Out of scope, explicitly.**

- Any clinical decision, triage, screening or diagnosis.
- Detecting, excluding, or characterising any fetal abnormality. The model
  segments a skull outline; it has no notion of pathology.
- Measuring anything other than head circumference. Biparietal diameter,
  occipitofrontal diameter, abdominal circumference and femur length are not
  supported, and HC is not a substitute for them.
- Images outside the transventricular plane. The model will still emit a
  number on an incorrect plane, and that number will be wrong in a way it
  cannot detect.
- First-trimester dating, where crown-rump length is the appropriate
  measurement.
- Any modality other than 2-D B-mode ultrasound.

**Intended users.** Researchers and engineers evaluating the pipeline. Not
sonographers, not clinicians, not patients.

---

## Data

**Training and evaluation:** HC18 Grand Challenge (Radboud University Medical
Center, Nijmegen, Netherlands) — 2-D transventricular fetal ultrasound with
expert HC annotations and per-image pixel spacing.

**Known distribution limits.** HC18 is single-centre, acquired on a limited
range of scanner hardware, from one geographic population. Performance on other
scanners, other populations, other operators, or images with different
acquisition settings is **unmeasured**, and single-centre medical imaging models
are known to degrade across sites. Treat any number here as an upper bound.

**In this repository:** 12 demo images plus `training_set_pixel_size_and_HC.csv`
(reference HC and pixel spacing). The full HC18 dataset is not redistributed.

**Cine mode uses synthesised motion.** Temporal variants (`phase2`, `phase4b`)
require a 16-frame clip. There is no cine input; the loop is synthesised from
the single frame (Pseudo-LDDM v2 — Ornstein-Uhlenbeck probe motion, Rician
speckle, depth attenuation). The inter-frame reliability score therefore
measures *stability under synthetic perturbation*, which is a precision proxy,
**not** agreement across a real acquisition.

## Limitations

**Measurement.**

- HC accuracy depends entirely on the supplied pixel spacing. A wrong spacing
  scales the result linearly, and the system cannot detect this — the mask can
  be perfect and the millimetre value badly wrong.
- The ellipse is fitted to the predicted mask, so a partial or broken skull
  segmentation produces an under- or over-estimate rather than a failure.
- Gestational age inherits every limitation of the Hadlock 1984 regression,
  including the population it was derived from and its widening prediction
  intervals later in pregnancy.

**Self-assessment.**

- For single-frame results, `reliability` and `hc_std_mm` are fixed
  placeholders (1.0 and 0.0), not measurements. They exist so the response
  shape matches cine mode. Consumers must read `mode` before interpreting them;
  the escalation agent does exactly this and seeks cross-checkpoint evidence
  instead.
- Inter-frame reliability is a *precision* statistic. Sixteen frames can agree
  closely on a measurement that is consistently wrong.

**Explainability.**

- GradCAM++ shows where the model's output was most sensitive. That is
  correlation with the model's own prediction, not evidence that the
  highlighted tissue caused the measurement.
- The attribution summary locates focus geometrically and relative to the
  predicted skull. It does **not** identify anatomical structures, and nothing
  in a saliency map could distinguish a real structure from a bright speckle
  artifact.

**Generated text.**

- LLM-generated report narratives, Q&A answers and escalation justifications
  are grounded in retrieved reference material and the measurement's own
  numbers, but are still model output and can be wrong.
- Several reference sections in `knowledge/` are marked provisional: they are
  paraphrase, not verbatim guideline text. The API flags these and the UI
  badges them.

**Evaluation.**

- No ground-truth segmentation masks ship with this repository, so the CI gate
  measures HC error, not Dice.
- Twelve demo images is a regression signal, not an accuracy estimate.
- No subgroup analysis has been performed. Performance across gestational age
  bands, image quality tiers, maternal habitus or scanner models is unmeasured.

## Ethical and safety considerations

- **Automation bias.** A confident-looking number with an ellipse drawn on the
  image invites acceptance. The reliability verdict and abstention behaviour
  exist to counter this, and are themselves imperfect.
- **Silent failure is the main hazard.** The model produces a plausible number
  on an incorrect plane, a wrong pixel spacing, or an out-of-distribution
  image. The OOD check and quality score are heuristics, not guarantees.
- **No patient data** is stored. Findings are held in an in-memory LRU with a
  TTL and are lost on restart. Uploaded images are not written to disk.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/evaluate.py --out eval_results.json
python scripts/check_thresholds.py eval_results.json
```
