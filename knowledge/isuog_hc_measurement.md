## Correct plane for head circumference measurement

> Source: ISUOG Practice Guidelines — routine mid-trimester fetal ultrasound scan.
> TODO(verbatim): paste the exact plane-criteria wording from the guideline.

Head circumference is measured in a transverse axial plane of the fetal head.
The plane is conventionally identified by a symmetrical appearance of the two
cerebral hemispheres, a continuous midline falx interrupted anteriorly by the
cavum septi pellucidi, and the absence of the cerebellum from the image. The
cerebellum appearing in the plane indicates the section is angled too far
posteroinferiorly.

## How the HC ellipse is placed

> Source: ISUOG Practice Guidelines — biometry section.
> TODO(verbatim): confirm the outer-perimeter convention against the guideline
> text, since ellipse placement conventions differ between HC and BPD.

Head circumference is taken around the **outer perimeter of the calvarium**.
It does not include the scalp or other soft tissue external to the skull bone.
This differs from biparietal diameter, whose measurement convention depends on
whether an outer-to-inner or outer-to-outer caliper placement is being used, so
the two measurements should not be assumed to share a convention.

## Ellipse versus two-diameter derivation of HC

> Source: ISUOG Practice Guidelines — biometry section.
> TODO(verbatim): paste the guideline's statement on the acceptability of
> the derived-HC formula versus direct ellipse tracing.

HC can be obtained either by placing an ellipse directly around the calvarium
or by deriving it from the biparietal and occipitofrontal diameters. The two
approaches do not always agree exactly, so a measurement should be interpreted
knowing which method produced it. This system measures the ellipse directly
from the predicted segmentation boundary.

## Image quality factors that invalidate an HC measurement

> Source: ISUOG Practice Guidelines — image quality / quality control.
> TODO(verbatim): paste the guideline's quality-criteria list.

An HC measurement should be treated as unreliable when the calvarium is
incompletely visualised, when shadowing from the near-field skull obscures the
boundary, when the head is compressed against the uterine wall or by the
transducer, or when the image is acquired at an oblique angle rather than a
true axial plane. Gain and focus settings that blur the bone–soft-tissue
interface also degrade boundary placement.

## Interobserver variability in fetal biometry

> Source: TODO(verbatim) — cite the specific reproducibility study or the
> guideline's quality-control section. Do NOT quote a numeric tolerance here
> until it is sourced; the figure varies by study, gestation and operator.

Fetal biometric measurements carry measurement error from both the plane
selection and the caliper/ellipse placement. Repeated measurement of the same
fetus by different operators, or by the same operator on different images,
yields a spread rather than a single value. Any single HC value should
therefore be read as an estimate with an associated uncertainty, not an exact
quantity. A specific acceptable tolerance should be taken from the guideline or
local protocol in force, not assumed.

## Why gestational age from biometry is not a dating method late in pregnancy

> Source: ISUOG / ACOG dating guidance.
> TODO(verbatim): paste the dating-hierarchy statement.

Gestational age estimated from second- or third-trimester biometry is less
accurate than first-trimester crown–rump length dating, because biological
variation in fetal size increases with gestation. Once a pregnancy has been
dated by an earlier scan or by a reliable last menstrual period, that dating is
normally not revised on the basis of later biometry. A biometry-derived GA is
therefore an assessment of fetal size relative to expected, not a redating.
